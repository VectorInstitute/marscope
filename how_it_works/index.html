    How it works¶

    MARScope is a pipeline around three ONNX detectors. The detectors are unchanged research checkpoints; what MARScope adds is everything around the score: the frames are prepared exactly as in training, the score is placed among the model's test videos, the model is tested, and fixed rules explain the result. This page walks through what happens between choosing a video and reading the report.

    The pipeline at a glance¶

    Video

    MP4, MOV or WebM. Checked first: faces, bitrate, length and Content Credentials.

    →

    Clips

    Every second frame, in 64-frame clips, prepared exactly as in training.

    →

    Models

    MARS, GenD and DFD-HR score each clip (ONNX Runtime, GPU or CPU).

    →

    Context and tests

    Each score placed among the model's test videos; A1, A4 and A5 re-score the strongest clips.

    →

    Notes and report

    Fixed, cited notes from deterministic rules; a report with optional LLM prose.

    Frames and clips¶

    The models only give meaningful scores on input prepared the way they were trained, so this part never changes:

    • Frames are decoded as RGB with PyAV, and every second frame is kept.
    • Kept frames are grouped in clips of 64. A short last clip is padded by repeating its last frame, and only its real frames count.
    • Each frame is resized to a square (bicubic, no crop) and scaled to [-1, 1].
    • A clip score is the model's probability of fake for that clip. The video score is the mean over clips.

    Clip length, frame stride and image size are read from each model's ONNX metadata, not written in the code. On a GPU the models run in fp16 (the same results as the paper); on the CPU, in fp32.

    Scores in context¶

    A raw score means little: one model may give most real videos 10% and another 50%. So each model comes with its usual ranges: the scores it gave the benchmark test videos (11,098 real, 12,435 fake), stored as 101 percentiles for each precision.

    • "Very unusual for a real video: fewer than 1 in 100 real videos score this high" says where the score sits among the model's real test videos, in plain odds. The app draws it as a bar: the band is where real videos usually score (from the lowest 5% to the highest 5%), the dot is this video.
    • A high fake score is one above the model's usual range for real videos (its 95th percentile). It describes the score; nothing is decided by it.
    • Models are compared by that place, never by raw score. The result says whether the models agree, and how consistent the evidence is (consistent, mixed or weak).

    Evidence¶

    • MARS has built-in evidence: how much each frame and each area (a 24 × 24 grid) adds to its score. The app draws it on the frame and on the timeline.
    • GenD and DFD-HR have no built-in map. An occlusion map hides each area of an 8 × 8 grid in turn and measures how the score changes.
    • Tests against chance. "Hide its top frames" (or areas) removes what the model ranks highest and compares with removing as many at random. If the top picks move the score much more than random ones, the model relies on them.

    Evidence describes what a model relies on, never where a forgery is.

    The assumptions, A1 to A5¶

    Every detector rests on assumptions about what gives a fake away. MARScope asks five of them as questions, answered per model with measured numbers:

    Question How it is answered
    A1 Does it break when the face outline (the seam of a face swap) is hidden? The face outline is blanked in the strongest clips, which are scored again. Needs a face
    A2 Does it break on a new generator? The model's results on generators it never saw (GenVideo, DF26)
    A3 Does it hold on videos unlike its training data? The checks before the models: faces, bitrate, length, shape
    A4 Does it break when the video is shared many times? The strongest clips at 40% resolution, light blur and noise, JPEG quality 35
    A5 Does it break when fine pixel detail is removed? The strongest clips with their fine detail removed

    The answer is judged against the model's own usual range for real videos:

    • Holds: the score stays where it was.
    • Partly: it falls, but stays higher than the model usually gives real videos.
    • Breaks: it falls into the model's usual range for real videos, so the model needed what the test removed.

    Notes¶

    Deterministic rules turn the measured values into notes, in English or French:

    • How far to trust the models: A1–A5, per model.
    • Where the video comes from: whether the file carries Content Credentials (C2PA), and what they say. Missing credentials prove nothing either way.
    • Laws and risks that may apply: labelling rules in the EU, intimate-image laws in Canada and the US, and the deepfake risks listed in the MIT AI Risk Repository.

    Rules never change a score, give the same notes for the same video and versions, and attach their own references. Settings are provisional until they are checked on the test sets.

    The report¶

    The report is filled from a template: every number, note and reference comes from the result. A language model can write the connecting summary (optional, see Reports and Ask). It receives the result in words, the notes without numbers and a plain description of your tests, never the video or frames. Sentences with a number or a conclusion about the video, and references the rules did not attach, are removed; if anything fails, the template summary is used and the report says so.

    Ask¶

    General questions are answered from a small set of reviewed knowledge cards, written from our survey, the MARS paper and the MOZAIC benchmark, and found with a plain keyword search (no model, no network). Questions about a result go to the language model, if one is set up, with the result's facts and the closest cards; the same filter as the report applies. A question asking whether the video is real or fake always gets the fixed answer that leaves the decision to you.

    Privacy¶

    The video and its frames stay on the machine that runs MARScope. A job and its files are deleted an hour after last use, or when you leave the result. The only outside calls are the model downloads from Hugging Face and, if you set one up, the language model.
